Hello everyone, I am SIU Tak-Leung, and I am announcing here that I am running in the 2027 Hong Kong Special Administrative Region Chief Executive Election.

This is earlier than usual, but since I currently have no support at all, I have to start promoting early. If you remember me, I also ran in the previous election, but why do you rarely see me campaigning? The reason is that today's media only reports what it likes — naked runs, sex scandals, celebrity feuds — these are the daily mainstays of media coverage, while they rarely cover things that would make society better. This is not a joke: Hong Kong media will not seriously consider what would actually make Hong Kong a more cultured, civilised place, and would rather chase sensationalism to grab eyeballs. But I will not go against the current, so this time I will use exaggerated tactics to cater to Hong Kong media tastes, in order to secure publicity for myself.

This time I will run as a scientist; what I demand is reasonable facts. The campaign slogan will be "Use Your Brain" (「用吓腦」; the Mandarin equivalent is more genteel: 「動腦筋」). I have already opened a Facebook group, you are welcome to join the discussion. Because Facebook and various groups have a habit of suppressing free speech, I have multiple accounts, since I've been locked out many times before. If you want to distinguish whether an account is really me, I suggest checking whether what the account says uses its brain — if not, don't bother reading, it's fake!

> *Translator's note: Throughout the campaign the candidate uses the slogan 「用吓腦」, which captures the Cantonese colloquial register and is left untranslated here. In Mandarin the equivalent would be 「動腦筋」, which sounds more formal.*

Last election, people said I used an old manifesto, and we ended up electing a Chief Executive with no manifesto at all. You can see that many people do not understand politics: they think that belief and ideals alone can make society better. Like in Western elections — of the manifestos promised, how many are actually delivered? Most don't even reach half, and some even destroy the foundations built by previous generations, wasting time and resources. I will not play this game. I am a scientist — simple and direct — and I will do my utmost to lead Hong Kong to become the wisest place it has ever been. This is not empty talk: as long as you read through the entire manifesto, you will understand.

**Why am I running?**

The main reason is that Hong Kong's governing team is staffed by a bunch of incompetent people. The Hong Kong I see has had two things severely destroyed — the higher culture that Chinese civilisation has upheld for thousands of years: wisdom and morality. The first principle of my term as Chief Executive would be to restore these two things.

In recent years we can see how officials nurtured during British rule govern by saying "I don't know", "I can't see it", "I don't remember", "I believe so", and "it's not my problem". This trend is worldwide — as I said in the previous election, dumbing down makes people easier to govern, so education everywhere tends toward dumbing down. Today's Hong Kong has perfectly absorbed all the bad habits left over from British rule. You see the world split into two sides, but whichever side you stand on, it pushes society toward war and makes the poor poorer. Why is this so? Because this is exactly the product of the dumbing-down era: pitting people against each other keeps the regime stable, doesn't challenge the rulers' wisdom, but it also manufactures social conflict.

During the British colonial period, Hong Kong was given an education system that even the British themselves do not use — teaching-to-the-test education (填鴨教育, "duck-stuffing education" — forcing knowledge into students without digestion). Teaching-to-the-test education only emphasises knowledge and neglects virtue and wisdom, turning Hong Kong people into puppets who cannot tell right from wrong and only know how to follow orders. I propose three main culture-and-education shifts:

> *Translator's note: 填鴨 literally means "stuffing the duck" and is the standard Hong Kong/Taiwan Chinese term for forced-feeding pedagogy — i.e. cramming knowledge into students without digestion.*

1. **"Love" upgraded to "Morality"**: The most brainwashed thing in Western education is "love". People today can no longer tell the difference between "love" and "morality"; they assume having love equals high moral conduct. Actually, just look at how many disputes the "priority seats" (關愛座 — designated seats for the elderly, pregnant, disabled, etc.) generate, and you will see how "love" destroys society. A culture that talks about love is actually quite low-grade.

> *Translator's note: The "priority seat" (關愛座) controversy is a recurring flashpoint in Hong Kong public transport — passengers argue over who deserves the seat, footage often goes viral. The candidate uses this as a concrete example of how "love" (愛心) without judgment becomes a vehicle for self-righteousness and conflict.*

2. **"Rules" upgraded to "Principles"**: It has been a long time since anyone talked about having principles in life. All we see is either rule-following or demanding freedom, with no flexibility at all. Teaching-to-the-test education has turned Hong Kong into a place that goes by the book, disregarding consequences and disregarding purpose, simply following rules for the sake of following them. There is even a low-grade culture that treats "following rules" as equivalent to "having principles".

3. **"Knowledge" upgraded to "Wisdom"**: Everyone has heard the phrase "high score, low ability" (高分低能) — it describes someone who has "knowledge" but no "wisdom". How to lift knowledge up to wisdom: this is what I will focus on this term. In this area, I can say: no one else will do.

> *Translator's note: 高分低能 is a common Cantonese/Hong Kong expression used to criticise an education system that produces test-passing but life-incompetent graduates.*

Let me elaborate on points 1 and 3. First, "morality". For example, more and more children are being bullied in schools, and there are students throwing themselves from buildings. I blame the fact that today's school principals are not chosen for being moral people but for being people of advantage. Such an approach, of course, cannot solve bullying. But even though I am using exaggerated tactics to promote this time, there are some things that simply cannot be done — I cannot change society's moral concepts in a short time, as the saying goes, "it takes ten years to grow a tree, a hundred years to cultivate a person". Of course I could enact a "morality law" and make non-compliance illegal, but that approach would quickly be exploited in small ways, and would not achieve good results until society collapses. Morality can only be taught, but teaching cannot happen overnight, so I would lay the foundation during my term and let people move in the right direction. To build the moral foundation, another thing is essential: we cannot casually talk about "love" or "kindness"; we must rely on the "wisdom" I will discuss later to lift love up to morality.

As for wisdom, I can educate the public in a short time. I borrow Chan Buddhist terminology: point directly to the mind, see one's nature and attain enlightenment; seeing one's nature is sudden awakening. How to point directly to the mind? As I said before, education worldwide tends to dumb people down — the result is today's society that only follows the book. Any attempt to change is treated as rule-breaking and suppressed, so society cannot progress. Instead, amid endless conflicts, people fight each other and society slides into decline. Hong Kong's economy has gone from a solid foundation to living on debt. Before bankruptcy it can still look glamorous, the regime appearing stable, but by the time of bankruptcy it is too late to fix. The vested interests and the wise emigrate; those who can't leave remain and suffer.

I personally am very afraid of, and hate, living in a place governed by incompetent people. I despise unreasonable things — for example, promoting legal voting being treated as illegal; a traffic case lasting 91 days for an accidental jaywalking; a motorcyclist who died after being obstructed, with footage available but no evidence found. Even more outrageous, a bureau chief being interviewed and asked how much of 13 billion had been used, the answer was something like "the subtlety is that we don't know how much was used". This kind of evasive twirling expression is really just "I won't tell you", and what I read into it is that you don't want to account, because you are ashamed of what you did, or perhaps you are ashamed of not having done anything or of anything being successful. Faced with officials who bear zero responsibility, I really think Hong Kong has failed — and worse, I see more and more of these kinds of people and officials in Hong Kong.

To make a place strong, I think of a Western film, Hidden Figures (關鍵少數), where Black mathematicians make clear the situation to the head of NASA, and the head changes policy to make NASA stronger. I am now playing the role of the Black mathematician, but I am not Galileo — I don't know the powerful people who could protect me from being stoned to death. I cannot demand that all Hong Kong people have wisdom, knowing how to think for society and how to grow stronger. So if this time I still cannot get publicity opportunities, I only hope to be allowed to leave, to live in another place where I can safely speak reasonable things. All I ask is to come back sometimes to visit family. That would be enough.

> *Translator's note: Hidden Figures (2016) tells the story of Black female mathematicians at NASA during the Space Race, and the institutional racism they overcame. The Galileo reference alludes to his house arrest for defending heliocentrism. The 130 billion figure, the 91-day traffic case, and the motorcyclist's death correspond to specific Hong Kong news stories the candidate cites as examples of governance failure.*

**What qualifies me?**

Many doubt what qualifications I have to hold the office of Chief Executive. In terms of qualification, I believe my level of loving-the-country-and-loving-Hong-Kong (愛國愛港) would only exceed others'. First, I will donate half of my income back to society. I have never seen a so-called patriot who is willing to give up their own long-term interests. What I have instead seen is calling on officials to take pay cuts and share Hong Kong's hardship — they only raise taxes, issue more parking tickets, add waste charges, and push the problem to society. If anyone thinks that my donating half my salary to charity is still not as patriotic as this team that "can't bear to fall apart at all" (「少個崩都唔制嘅」) — feel free to speak up, I enjoy meaningful discussions.

> *Translator's note: "愛國愛港人士" (patriots who love Hong Kong) is the official term used by the Hong Kong government since the 2020 electoral reform to describe the political screening criterion for candidates and officials. The phrase 「少個崩都唔制嘅」 is colloquial Cantonese sarcasm — roughly "couldn't bear to break even one corner" — used here mockingly about the governing class.*

**What am I capable of?**

You can see what this term's Chief Executive is capable of: escaping reality, sitting around slowly eroding society. In this respect, I really don't have that kind of capability. As a scientist, my only ability is to lead everyone in understanding things they previously did not know or did not see. Of course I am not a magician: I cannot open the eyes of those who cannot see, nor give a brain to those who don't have one. Some of you may think I am talking nonsense, but I can tell you: even Shing-Tung Yau (丘成桐) would only produce incompetent students. Spending hundreds of millions setting up a math institute ends up producing test-passing-but-life-incompetent graduates. Why? Because the books they read never teach what wisdom is.

I will start with 1+1=1+1, to re-teach everyone to recognise facts, and to add it into primary and secondary school textbooks. Under my leadership, Hong Kong can well become the wisest city in the world.

From my experience in the previous election, Hong Kong people and the media only look at my first manifesto, and afterwards pay less and less attention. So please read through this entire manifesto this time — after reading, you will know exactly how Hong Kong people can gain in wisdom.

> *Translator's note: Shing-Tung Yau is the Fields Medal–winning Chinese-American mathematician. The candidate uses him as an example of how even world-class mathematical talent, if reduced to "teaching knowledge" without wisdom, still produces the same teaching-to-the-test outcomes.*

**My political stance**

I am politically apathetic and neutral. I will only focus on how to make people's lives better and happier, not on how to regulate everything, and certainly not on being like today's government which fails at everything except national security. As I said before, I hope to have a governing team that uses its brain to help me, rather than fighting alone. Fighting alone is pointless — only when everyone uses wisdom to solve problems and makes people happy does it have meaning. Calling every day for this or that to be guarded against, of course some individual benefits, but society as a whole does not progress. Set down disputes and hatred, build an ideal and secure place for the future — that is the point. Even if I can bring wisdom to everyone, without a stable place, where is the room to exercise power? "Facts must be clearly seen, hatred must be set down" — that is my way of resolving disputes.

> *Translator's note: The candidate's line 「事實要認清，仇恨要放低」 is the campaign's stated motto for reconciliation. It deliberately echoes the structure of martial-arts/moral slogans common in Chinese political rhetoric, signalling its keynesian/rhetorical role in the manifesto.*

I believe political struggles should end here, and instead struggle for the happiness of the people. After I become Chief Executive, I will adopt a "great reconciliation" approach. The 721 incident, the 47-person case — everyone involved in these, I will seek lenient treatment for all of them. Especially Claudia Ho (何桂藍): I watched the 721 incident live and saw her being beaten, so I think she should receive leniency. I will reference two precedents: first, a traffic police officer who ran a red light causing a person to have both legs amputated, but multiple senior police figures wrote mitigation letters; second, Lee Lu (李祿) can return to mainland China and live there. I will draw on these two cases to make representations to the court. Do not do to others what you do not want done to yourself — I do not want legal acts becoming crimes, and the 47-person case happened before the law was passed, so I will make representations to the court. However, for Jimmy Lai (黎智英), Benny Tai (戴耀廷), and Joshua Wong (黃之鋒), I will not seek leniency, because I will only do what is good for society. Likewise, assaulting police and using violence will not receive leniency — they should be punished. The person who rode a motorcycle into a police formation has already shown remorse and luckily did not harm anyone, so I think that case can also be handled leniently.

> *Translator's note: The 721 incident refers to the 21 July 2019 Yuen Long attack. The 47-person case refers to the prosecution of 47 pro-democracy figures (including Claudia Ho / Ho Kai-Lam) for subversion under the National Security Law, whose trial ran from early 2023. The candidate is signalling a blanket amnesty position. The two precedent cases cited are: (a) a 2018 traffic officer who injured a pedestrian, and (b) the controversial case of Lee Lu (a.k.a. "Standing", reportedly convicted of riot-related offences) who the candidate claims was allowed to return to mainland China. The 130 billion reference is to a specific government programme whose spending details the candidate argues officials refused to disclose. Names are transliterated according to common Romanisation conventions.*

To unite the people, I do not need to fabricate enemies — because I have already grasped the ultimate secret of human wisdom, and I am not afraid of being challenged by anyone. Anyone who wants to fight, can face me and my government; receiving criticism makes citizens' lives better — that is wisdom. Of course teaching citizens to condemn the government is absolutely not my purpose; my purpose is to teach citizens to demand higher moral conduct, rather than criticism without direction. What I need even more is a group of people who know how to use their brain, to join me in educating this dumbed-down age. I will integrate the highest wisdom into nature, advancing in the most natural way, rather than blindly charging forward.

You should now have a preliminary understanding of my philosophy of political management. If you want to understand the issues in greater depth, feel free to join my public Facebook group for discussion — I will answer personally.

**Now to the main topic — my platform.**

Earlier I mentioned that some people had a problem with me using an old manifesto. I believe Hong Kong's problems are not from the past year or two — they are the accumulation of decades, so what I write is applicable any time. But since some have asked, I will satisfy them by organising a manifesto for this campaign and including a core plan.

> *Translator's note: This echoes the candidate's stated complaint from the previous campaign — that the public/media reduced his first manifesto to a single sound bite and then ignored the platform. The current manifesto length and detail is in part a deliberate response.*

Man proposes, Heaven disposes (謀事在人，成事在天). This time my campaign slogan is "Use Your Brain".

Why is society in turmoil and the people restless? The biggest problem is, of course, in governance. From the 1997 handover to the present, the governing class has been unqualified. The British government used teaching-to-the-test education to create a governing class with no moral requirements, no ability requirements, and a target of bootlicking. In the old days, the disciplined services had to display portraits of the so-called "Queen Boss" (事頭婆) in prominent places to please foreigners for the sake of promotion — and of course that pleased foreign superiors, while harming citizens. After the handover, this official mentality did not change: officials act merely to please their superiors, not to "use their brain" to solve problems.

> *Translator's note: 「事頭婆」 is a colloquial Cantonese nickname for Queen Elizabeth II, used both affectionately and irreverently. The portrait-display practice refers to the British colonial requirement that government offices display the Queen's portrait.*

**Core plan: "Hong Kong Is the World, the World Is Hong Kong"**

I will also add a new element to this manifesto this time. Beyond the previous manifesto, I will include a core plan — to launch the "Hong Kong Is the World, the World Is Hong Kong" plan. With the help of AI and Hong Kong's financial strength, the vision of Hong Kong people will look out at the globe, building a community of shared future for mankind, instead of being trapped in a small place now. Beyond the Belt and Road Initiative, I will cooperate with every place. Hong Kong people can live and work abroad, using Hong Kong as a central node, focusing wisdom and technology to connect with the whole world — through tight linkages including legal and financial — to spread Hong Kong to other places. In computing, there is a term: distributed system. I will make Hong Kong a distributed Hong Kong. Of course this part will need central government support. After all, there are many places in the world competing with Hong Kong — they will not use cooperation to achieve a win-win, but will use monopolies to make other places fail, even when they themselves can't win. This is the kind of competition in the world, and for Hong Kong to be a world centre, political neutrality is essential — no participation in great-power competition, only seeking multi-win cooperation. The previous example of Switzerland, declaring itself neutral, attracted unconditional capital inflows from around the world. That is why support from the central government is so important.

> *Translator's note: The "Belt and Road Initiative" (一帶一路) is China's signature transnational infrastructure programme. The "community of shared future for mankind" (人類命運共同體) is an official PRC diplomatic concept promoted since 2013. The candidate is positioning the Hong Kong plan as complementary — but politically neutral — to these Chinese state initiatives.*

**Basic development: "Use Your Brain" for sustainable Hong Kong development**

1. Hong Kong is only suited to political neutrality, with the economy as the focus. Drawing on the Olympic arrangement model, establish a crime of insulting any head of state, attracting capital from around the world and making Hong Kong an administrative capital. Do not participate in any form of political movement; only provide a stable and high-quality living environment. Do not seek a democratic system, nor practise communism. Do not pursue equality for all; only emphasise that citizens enjoy a peaceful life.

> *Translator's note: The Olympic reference points to the Olympic Charter Rule 50, which prohibits political demonstrations inside Olympic venues — used here as a model of "no political activity" neutrality.*

2. Build a global village. Relax immigration to Hong Kong. Anyone who wants to contribute to Hong Kong society gets a chance to come in. The condition is contribution to Hong Kong; use total tax paid as the criterion, replacing residency years. Before naturalisation, an annual 50% of income will be collected as tax. Once the target is reached, naturalisation can be granted, after which the welfare and tax regime is the same as Hong Kong people.

3. For those who do not identify with Hong Kong's governing system and its values, I will also fight for their right to live in different places, so they can go and find a place that suits them.

4. Research the establishment of sister cities outside Hong Kong territory. Within Hong Kong, integrate different cultures; combine the different features of sister cities; simplify immigration procedures and visas; simplify the tax regime to expand Hong Kong; subsidise development in other regions.

5. Each month, focus-promote a different culture. Increase introductions to festivals of different countries. Strengthen Hong Kong's international-city image. Add AI translations to all government websites into different country languages.

6. Review the use of social resources. Previously, those who obtained social resources were first the famous-and-good ones, then those who obtained resources were the famous-and-bad ones, and only at the end were the hard-working ordinary ones — yet these people contribute the most to society. I will change the strategy: those who receive social resources must have a limited time to reciprocate to society, rather than emigrating away after receiving resources, or continuing to erode society. Cultivating talent with government resources must make society better, not serve personal enjoyment.

7. Introduce foreign consular representatives into LegCo, with multilingual real-time translation. Sister cities can join in deliberating Hong Kong legislation. Although the numbers are small and this is only a formal expression of position, it conveys that Hong Kong is an international city, and increases Hong Kong legislators' exposure to global cultures and views.

> *Translator's note: LegCo is the abbreviation for Legislative Council, Hong Kong's legislature. "Foreign consular representatives" sitting in LegCo would be highly unusual in normal constitutional terms — the candidate is signalling a symbolic/translational model, not a literal seat.*

**Education: Increase the weight of subjects based on practical wisdom; let's all "Use Your Brain".**

1. Chinese language: Use the Six-Principles-of-character-formation (六書 — the traditional Chinese philological classification of characters into pictographic, indicative, compound-ideographic, phonetic-compound, derivative, and borrowed) as the guiding principle of Chinese education. Reduce translations of foreign words that have no Chinese meaning. Hope to reduce the worship-of-foreign mentality, and learn to use Chinese to understand things.

2. Teaching-to-the-test education: Redefine the purpose of education. Promote science and mathematics. Merge the Space Museum, Science Museum, Hong Kong Observatory, and other scientific resources into a unified way to provide scientific information to citizens.

3. Cost: Review the fact that Hong Kong's education spending is among the highest in the world.

4. Morality: The contemporary world seems to no longer mention the importance of morality. Even university professors and school principals do not consider "having morality" a hiring requirement — this is the main cause of society's decline. I will restore the City Forum (城市論壇 — a long-running open public discussion forum in Hong Kong, originally hosted by RTHK), allowing educators to discuss what morality is, where the line falls between morality, public duty, and personal interest. Cancel all government "Priority Seats" (the dedicated seats for elderly, pregnant, etc.). Use educational methods to promote that anyone can assess their own ability and help others. All public places should be non-smoking, including streets and parks. All private areas, malls and restaurants should clearly mark at the door whether they are smoking areas. If any mall or restaurant has special conditions (such as tipping or dress codes), they must be clearly marked at the entrance; if not marked, follow no-tipping and Hong Kong standard practice.

> *Translator's note: City Forum (城市論壇) is a famous weekly Sunday open discussion event at the Hong Kong Public Square in Kowloon Tong, hosted since the 1980s by RTHK. Many political figures, academics, and activists have participated. It was suspended during 2019 protests and resumed later — its restoration is itself a politically loaded proposal.*

5. Sports: Reform the elite-athlete system. Change the mentality of using sport for gambling or competition. Sport is for fitness and entertainment. Increase fitness and nutrition courses in school physical-education lessons.

6. Reform or add an examination scoring system — e.g. compare against personal best, not against others. Increase education options, including fixed tutoring classes / AI tutoring classes, open to students from any class without segregation.

7. Refer to foreign waste/environmental practices, prioritising economy, to build environmental awareness among Hong Kong people.

8. Educate district councillors to "use their brain" to manage their own districts. Don't push everything to the government — you too are part of the government.

> *Translator's note: District councillors (區議員) are the elected representatives at the sub-municipal level in Hong Kong. They were reformed under 2023 electoral changes.*

**All-weather living environment: "Use Your Brain" — more freedom and convenience!**

1. During day/night and during Typhoon Signal No. 8, there must be sufficient public transport. Change the old view that typhoons mean staying home. Increase personal freedom to work and rest. Promote work-shift systems and modern communication tools.

2. Relax rules on personal-mobility electric vehicles. Electric vehicles under 5 m/s may use pedestrian paths. The principle is personal mobility; local decisions by district councillors.

3. Progressively extend public-facility opening hours, opening 24-hour public services where needed.

4. Abolish the statutory working-hours regime. Instead, employment contracts must specify working hours and overtime pay.

5. Abolish the HK$2 public transport scheme, replace it with an increase in Old Age Living Allowance.

> *Translator's note: The HK$2 public transport concession is a Hong Kong government subsidy letting elderly and disabled persons ride designated public transport for HK$2 per trip. Old Age Living Allowance (生果金) is a monthly cash allowance for elderly residents. The candidate is signalling the change from transport-specific subsidies to direct cash transfers, a notable policy shift.*

**Governance policy revisions: "Use Your Brain" — increase revenue, decrease expenditure**

1. Develop satellite cities and use modern technology to extend Hong Kong's virtual space, with the goal of broadening the tax base.

2. Review the tax rate of foreign companies in Hong Kong. Research increasing taxation of non-Hong-Kong residents. The principle is unchanged personal income tax. Instead of Hong Kong earnings going to foreign taxes, Hong Kong earnings should contribute more to Hong Kong taxes.

3. Research the feasibility of global taxation: collect tax from non-Hong-Kong-resident persons.

4. Change annual taxation to monthly taxation, on a pilot basis.

5. Luxury goods such as tobacco and alcohol: the principle is the highest tax revenue the government can collect. Tax rates change yearly according to citizens' paying ability. Personal freedom to use luxury goods is not restricted.

6. Reduce monopolies, open the market. Fully open clothing, food, housing, transport to the market. Stop interest transfers. Maximise tax revenue.

7. Tier the laws. For minor offences (e.g. littering or jaywalking), allow one warning-free period per month. Reduce frontline enforcement difficulty.

8. Reduce excessive expenditure. Review the functions and needs of the 13 bureaux. Avoid the absurdity of supervisory expenditure exceeding work expenditure — "one person works, ten supervise".

> *Translator's note: The Hong Kong government currently has 15 policy bureaux (局) under the Policy Innovation and Co-ordination Office structure; the reference to "13 bureaux" reflects an older (pre-2022) structure when this manifesto was originally written.*

9. Review the capability of the Hospital Authority.

10. Prohibit government donations to other disaster areas. Replace outright grants with low-interest loans, reducing the misuse of resources. Encourage private donations, but do not presumptuously donate on behalf of all citizens.

**Free services: "Use Your Brain" — what citizens genuinely need as a basic, with the government able to pay**

1. Provide free AI-based Chinese to foreign-language translation, including for websites and APIs, lowering the threshold for Hong Kong's communication with the world. Research the use of IPFS or BitTorrent to reduce network speed needs.

2. Merge all government websites and mobile apps into one entry portal. Separate local citizens from foreign visitors. Local citizens' side focuses on utility; visitors' side focuses on image promotion. All pages support AI translation to other languages.

3. Provide free meals in low-income fringe areas, only at survival-standard quality. Eliminate the old-era notion of "no work means you'll starve." Anyone in Hong Kong, even without work, will not starve to death. But work allows a better quality of life.

4. Provide free low-speed internet across Hong Kong, with text messaging as the standard. Research free use of 802.11ah (a low-power Wi-Fi standard for IoT/long-range coverage) or low-speed GSM, so every person in every place can use government AI services for free.

That concludes this manifesto for now. If you have better proposals or want to understand more, please feel free to discuss in my open Facebook group.

> *Translator's note: 802.11ah (also called Wi-Fi HaLow) operates in the sub-1 GHz band, designed for low-power, long-range, low-data-rate communication. Its use here is for a city-wide low-speed free network for basic services, not as a replacement for high-speed broadband.*
